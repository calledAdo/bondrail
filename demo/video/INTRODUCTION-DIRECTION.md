What makes the reference work:

- 15–40s introduces a clear job, then follows its inputs. It avoids a feature catalogue.
- 40–62s shows a before/after transformation on the same board. Camera pulls out to establish the board, then pushes toward the changed figures. The moving screen remains readable; cuts follow narrative beats.
- 68–104s introduces a complication and resolves it. Email and board share the frame only when their relationship matters.
- 109–127s spotlights one integration at a time by muting neighbouring cards. The transition keeps spatial continuity.
- The closing returns to the original promise: what the conference trip produced. Short voice lines and restrained captions support the visual argument.

Avoid a permanent presentation header, tiny screens, repetitive title cards, or a constantly drifting camera. Original typography, motion, sound and artwork; do not reuse the reference’s assets, audio or branding.

## Story structure (target105–115s)

1. **0–15s — Problem.** An onchain business earns revenue over time, but needs capital for its next move today. Concrete costs: an audit, more capacity, a launch. Open on actual Bondrail homepage, then visualize the timing gap.
2. **15–24s — Bondrail.** Introduce revenue bonds and the basic promise. Page-to-symbol match cut, with money-flow lines beginning at the symbol's split.
3. **24–38s — Team.** Public names and roles, confirmed by the user. No invented credentials, portraits or personal origin story. Display names with readable typography over a quiet actual product/build context.
4. **38–52s — Why.** The user's actual motivation, expressed as a concrete belief or observed problem. Product hypothesis: an onchain revenue record should help a business raise for its next move; investors should be able to inspect the source and repayment rules.
5. **52–72s — Mechanism.** Businesses set terms, investors fund, revenue contributes to repayment. One continuous visual flow with short genuine UI inserts. Revenue bonds are capped revenue claims, not guaranteed returns.
6. **72–90s — Built and working.** Existing recorded issuance/activation/repayment from technical demo. Label Monad testnet and controlled demo activity. Native atomic splits only; do not imply all integrations settle this way.
7. **90–102s — First users.** Revenue-generating apps on Monad; founder outreach, assisted first issuance, and investors from each business's community. This is a plan, not claimed traction.
8. **102–112s — Close.** Return to the business’s next move. Bondrail: issue bonds, raise capital, repay investors from revenue. End on brand and product URL.
